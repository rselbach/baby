Keeping your Mac awake · click the baby

When the baby cries, your Mac stays up.

Baby lives in your menu bar. Click it and your Mac won't sleep or turn off the display until you click again, a timer runs out, or the job that asked finishes.

Download for macOS

Apple silicon, macOS 14 or later

Three ways to keep it awake

Each one holds a lease. Baby keeps the Mac on while any lease is held and lets it sleep once the last one ends.

Baby: keeping your Mac awake
Click

Until you click again

Left-click the icon to start, and again to stop. A click never ends a job's lease. The baby cries while sleep is blocked and dozes off when it isn't.

Right-click

For a set time

Choose Activate for, then Permanent or one of your presets. Add durations like 45 minutes in Settings.

$ baby run -- make test
$ baby hold --pid 48211
$ baby status --json

Terminal

For as long as a job runs

Scripts and coding agents take a lease that ends when their command or process exits.

The rest

Named leases

The menu lists who is keeping the Mac awake, like “Greendale CI” or “Nightly backup until 11:30 PM”, and releases any one of them.

Command line tool

Install it from Settings to link baby into ~/.local/bin. It opens Baby when needed, and baby help lists every option.

Login and launch

Start Baby at login, and have it start preventing sleep the moment it opens.

Never stuck awake

Baby holds macOS power assertions while a lease is held, and macOS drops them if Baby quits or crashes. Your own lease keeps the display on. A job's lease keeps the Mac running and lets the screen sleep.

Signed updates

Baby reads a signed feed and installs notarized releases without a trip to the browser.
